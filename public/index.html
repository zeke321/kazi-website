Kazi = “work” · “opportunity” in Swahili

Zero-interest loans for entrepreneurs in Tanzania and Chad

KAZI Startfunding finances small social and sustainable projects run by entrepreneurs who have no access to bank loans. KAZI's local NGOs select the projects and support the whole process, from the business plan to the repayment of the loan: field visits, administrative help and training workshops. Every repaid loan then finances the next project.

Our model

How a KAZI loan works

Questions? Read the FAQ →

Apply to the local NGO

Anyone who wishes to can contact KAZI Tanzania or FEDJIN KAZI Chad, present their project and apply for a loan.

Local selection

The local NGOs assess the viability and the social and environmental impact of the project, and help draw up a business plan.

Zero-interest loan + support

Funds are lent in local currency, and KAZI absorbs the exchange-rate risk. The local teams support the business: field visits, administrative help and training workshops.

Repay & re-lend

Repayments follow a set schedule and fund the next entrepreneur's project: the fund is revolving. Projects that have worked out can get a second loan.

About KAZI

Mission & model

KAZI Startfunding's mission is to create job opportunities that lead to financial and social independence in countries with limited resources, on a disinterested and non-profit basis.

The idea behind KAZI Startfunding is simple: provide financial support, administrative guidance and training workshops to people who want to start a social or sustainable project in Tanzania or Chad. Loans are indexed in local currency, so the people who borrow never carry the exchange-rate risk. Once repaid, the money is lent again to someone else: it is a revolving fund.

What every project must show

  • 📈Economic viability
  • 🤝A positive social impact
  • 🌱Respect for the environment
KAZI in context

From an idea to three local teams

KAZI Startfunding grew out of a series of encounters, a shared idea to reduce international inequality, and a collaboration between three entities: the KAZI Switzerland Association (2019), recognised as a public-interest organisation; the NGO KAZI Tanzania (2021); the NGO KAZI FEDJIN Chad (2023).
Legally separate, they work closely together on the basis of Memoranda of Understanding, written funding contracts and regular meetings.

Where KAZI operates

Local teams, close to the entrepreneurs

KAZI Tanzania, Ifakara

A non-profit NGO registered in Tanzania. The first field base since 2021, in the Kilombero Valley (Morogoro region): it selects the projects, follows them and trains the project leaders.

FEDJIN KAZI Chad

A non-profit NGO registered in Chad. The second field base, founded in 2023 by a Chadian committee, in the south-west of the country: the same work of selection, follow-up and training.

KAZI Switzerland, Geneva

A non-profit association registered in Switzerland. It handles fundraising and the Swiss administration.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Who can apply for a KAZI loan?

Entrepreneurs and groups in the areas where the local teams work (Ifakara in Tanzania, and south-western Chad) with a sustainable project that banks won't finance. The project must be economically viable and have a positive social and environmental impact. See how to apply.

Why are the loans at zero interest?

KAZI's goal is not profit but a fair start. At zero interest, entrepreneurs keep more of what their business earns and can invest it in growing. The zero rate is written into the statutes of all three entities.

Why are loans indexed in local currency?

Borrowers receive and repay their loan in Tanzanian shillings or CFA francs. KAZI absorbs the exchange-rate risk, so a fall in the local currency never increases what an entrepreneur owes.

Why a loan and not a donation?

A repaid loan funds the next entrepreneur's project: the fund is revolving, so each franc helps several projects over time. The local teams select every borrower carefully, and a good repayment record opens the door to a second loan.

How is my donation used?

Donations mainly go into the loan fund. Another share covers running costs (rent and salaries of the local teams; the committees are volunteers). Each year's activity report gives the detailed accounts. See the reports.

How do I become a member or join the Swiss committee?

Write to us at communication@kazi-funding.org and tell us how you would like to get involved. We will explain the options and next steps. More about getting involved.
